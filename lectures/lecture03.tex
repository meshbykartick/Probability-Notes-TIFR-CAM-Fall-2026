\section{Some Introductory Idea}

Suppose we toss a fair coin $n$-times.
\(
HTH\;HTTH\;\cdots\;T
\)


What is the Probability of the above sequence ? Its-  \(\sim \frac{1}{2^n}\)

If we have all sequences with $k$-heads then
\(
P(k\text{-heads})
=
\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{2^n},
\)
and it will be maximum when
\(
k=\frac{n}{2}.
\)
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

If
\(
x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_k
\)
are randomly independently chosen, then
\(
\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_i
\sim
\frac{r+1}{2}\cdot k,
\)
if they are chosen from the uniform distribution on
\(
\{1,2,\ldots,r\}.
\)

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Now Stirling's Formula,
\(
n!
\sim
\sqrt{2\pi n}
\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^n,
\)~~~
for very large $n$.
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Now,
\[
\frac{P(\text{number of heads }=k)}
     {P(\text{number of heads }=k+1)}
=
\frac{k+1}{n-k}.
\]

So,
\(
\frac{k+1}{n-k}>1
\iff
k>\frac{n-1}{2},
\)
and
\(
\frac{k+1}{n-k}<1
\iff
k<\frac{n-1}{2}.
\)

As
\(
\binom{n}{k}
\)
is maximum when
\(
k=\frac{n}{2},
\)
therefore,
\(
\frac{\binom{n}{n/2}}{2^n}
\sim
\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}
\qquad
\text{(By using Stirling's Formula).}
\)
\vspace{15pt}
It would be unjustified to say
\(
\#\text{Heads}=\frac{n}{2}
\)
with high probability.

Suppose
\(
k=\alpha n,
\qquad
\alpha\neq\frac12.
\)

Then
\(
\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{2^n}
\sim
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}
e^{\,n\left(
-\alpha\log\alpha
-(1-\alpha)\log(1-\alpha)
-\log2
\right)}.
\)

Let,
\(
H(\alpha)
=
-\alpha\log\alpha
-
(1-\alpha)\log(1-\alpha),
\)
be Boltzman Shanon Entropy (Mathematical theory of Communications.

Hence,
\[
\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{2^n}
\sim
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}
e^{\,n\left(
H(\alpha)-H\!\left(\frac12\right)
\right)}.
\]

Now,
\(
H(\alpha)
\le
H\!\left(\frac12\right),
\)
by using Jensen's inequality as \(\log~ \) is a concave function.

Equivalently,
\(
-\alpha\log\alpha
-
(1-\alpha)\log(1-\alpha)
<
\ln2.
\)

Therefore,
\(
\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{2^n}
\longrightarrow0
\)
exponentially.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Now,
\[
P\!\left(
\#\text{Heads}
\in
\bigl(
(\alpha-\varepsilon)n,
(\alpha+\varepsilon)n
\bigr)
\right)
=
\sum_{k=(\alpha-\varepsilon)n}^{(\alpha+\varepsilon)n}
\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{2^n}.
\qquad ,\text{where}
\varepsilon>0,
\qquad
\frac12
\notin
(\alpha-\varepsilon,\alpha+\varepsilon).
\]

Also,
\[
\frac{2n\varepsilon}{2^n}
\binom{n}{(\alpha-\varepsilon)n}
\le
\cdots
\le
\frac{2n\varepsilon}{2^n}
\binom{n}{(\alpha+\varepsilon)n}.
\]

Therefore, the asymptotic bounds are
\(
\frac{2n\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}
e^{\,n\left(
H(\alpha-\varepsilon)
-
H\!\left(\frac12\right)
\right)}
\)
and
\(
\frac{2n\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}
e^{\,n\left(
H(\alpha+\varepsilon)
-
H\!\left(\frac12\right)
\right)}.
\)


%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Therefore,
\(
P\!\left(
\#\text{Heads}
\in
\bigl(
(\alpha-\varepsilon)n,
(\alpha+\varepsilon)n
\bigr)
\right)
\longrightarrow0
\)
exponentially as
\(
n\to\infty.
\)

Hence,
\[
P\!\left(
\#\text{Heads}
\in
n\left(
\frac12-\varepsilon,
\frac12+\varepsilon
\right)
\right)
\longrightarrow1.
\]
This is called the \textbf{Large Deviation Phenomenon}.

\(
P(\text{staying close to mean})
\longrightarrow1,
\)

whereas
\(
P(\text{deviating from the mean significantly})
\longrightarrow0
\)
exponentially.


\vspace{15pt}
In our case, the rate function is
\(
H(\alpha)-H\!\left(\frac12\right).
\)
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Let
\(
f_n
=
\frac{\#\text{Heads}}{n}.
\)
Then
\(
f_n
\longrightarrow
f
\qquad
\text{in measure.}
\)

Therefore,

\[
P\!\left(
\left|
\frac{\#\text{Heads}}{n}
-
\frac12
\right|
>
\varepsilon
\right)
\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty.
\tag{1}
\]

We call this \textbf{convergence in probability}.

Later we shall use the \textbf{Borel--Cantelli Lemma} to prove from
(1) that
\(
\frac{\#\text{Heads}}{n}
\longrightarrow
\frac12
\)
almost surely.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The sample space is
\(
\Omega=\{H,T\}^{\mathbb N}.
\)
We equip \(\Omega\) with the product topology, where each copy of

\(\{H,T\}\) carries the discrete topology. The corresponding Borel
\(\sigma\)-algebra,
\(
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{B}(\Omega),
\)
is generated by the cylinder sets. The basis of the product topology consists of the cylinder sets, namely sets of the form
\(
\{\omega\in\Omega:
\omega_{i_1}=a_1,\ldots,\omega_{i_k}=a_k\}\),
where \( i_1,\ldots,i_k\) are finitely many coordinates and each
$a_j\in\{H,T\}$.

If we only specify the first n coordinates, they look like
$\{\omega:\omega_1=a_1,\ldots,\omega_n=a_n\}$,
which are the basic cylinders.

The complete probability space is
\(
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu\right)
=
\left(\{H,T\}^{\mathbb N},
\mathcal{B}\!\left(\{H,T\}^{\mathbb N}\right),
\mu\right),
\)

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Recall,

\[
P\!\left(
\#\text{Heads}
=
\frac n2
\right)
\sim
\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}.
\]

Suppose

\[
c>0,
\]

and let
\(
\left|
k-\frac n2
\right|
<
c\sqrt n.
\)

Then

\[
P(\#\text{Heads}=k)
=
\frac{
\dfrac{n!}{(n-k)!k!}
}{2^n}.
\]

Using Stirling's formula,

\[
P(\#\text{Heads}=k)
\sim
\frac{1}
{\sqrt{\pi n/2}}
\cdot
\frac{1}
{
\left(
2-\frac{2k}{n}
\right)^{\,n-k}
\left(
\frac{2k}{n}
\right)^k
}.
\]

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%


\[
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\cdot\frac{n}{4}}}
\cdot
\frac{1}{
\left(
1+\left(1-\frac{2k}{n}\right)
\right)^{n-k}
\left(
1+\left(\frac{2k}{n}-1\right)
\right)^k
}.
\]

Hence,

\[
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\cdot\frac{n}{4}}}
\exp\!\left(
-\frac{(n-2k)^2}{n}
\right).
\]

Equivalently, \(\qquad \qquad 
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\cdot\frac{n}{4}}}
\exp\!\left(
-\frac{\left(k-\frac n2\right)^2}
{2\cdot\frac n4}
\right),
\)
which resembles the normal distribution.

\[
\mu=\frac12,
\qquad
\sigma=\frac12.
\]

Hence,

\[
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2 n}}
\exp\!\left(
-\frac{(k-\mu n)^2}
{2\sigma^2 n}
\right).
\]

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We can prove,
\(
P\!\left(
a<
\frac{\#\text{Heads}-\mu n}
{\sigma\sqrt n}
<
b
\right)
\longrightarrow
\int_a^b
\varphi(x)\,dx,
\qquad
\text{as }n\to\infty.
\)
(\textbf{Central Limit Theorem / De Moivre--Laplace})

\section{Quiz, 10 August 2026 }

\begin{quiz}

\begin{enumerate}

    \item Let $p<\frac12$. Suppose we toss $n$ coins independently such
    that the probability of heads is $p$ and tails is $q=1-p$.
    
    For what value of $k$ is the probability of getting $k$ heads
    maximised?

    \item For probability vectors
    \(
    P=(p_1,p_2,\ldots,p_n),
    \qquad
    Q=(q_1,q_2,\ldots,q_n),
    \)
    where
    \(
    p_i,q_i>0,
    \qquad
    \sum_i p_i=\sum_i q_i=1,
    \)
    define the Kullback--Leibler divergence by
    \[
    D_{KL}(P\|Q)
    =
    \sum_i p_i\log\left(\frac{p_i}{q_i}\right).
    \]

    Use Jensen's inequality to show that
    \(
    D_{KL}(P\|Q)\geq 0
    \)
    with equality if and only if
    \(
    P=Q.
    \)

    \item Let $\alpha\in(0,1)$. Prove that the number of heads equalling
    $n\alpha$ is asymptotically
    \[
    \frac{1}
    {\sqrt{2\pi n\alpha(1-\alpha)}}
    \exp\left(
    -nD_{KL}(P\|Q)
    \right),
    \]
    where
    \(
    P=(\alpha,1-\alpha),
    \qquad
    Q=(p,q).
    \)

\end{enumerate}

\end{quiz}
